About me

Hi, I'm Kristina Van

I've emigrated three times since I was fourteen. That taught me three things: how to be at home in cultures that aren't mine, how to figure out money, relationships, health and work on my own, and how to talk to people in the language they actually experience the world in — without judging anything for being unfamiliar.

Professionally

Before psychology I ran a product business at 16, worked in modelling and talent management from 17 to 25, then media production and project management from 23 to 27. At 28, after covid and a personal crisis, I finally came to the thing I'd been circling since university.

I like taking up space. I get manic, anxious, foggy, tired. My biggest challenge is productivity and analysis that run well past the point of reasonable.

How I work

A clear frame, and a lot of freedom inside it

Integrative, with a psychodynamic lean. In Russian and English, through a feminist lens.

I listen to more than the words

Not only what you say, but how you say it, what you're telling me without words, and what you're not saying. I'm present both as a person in the conversation and in a meta-position — noticing what dynamic you bring and what unfolds between us.

I don't insist that I'm right

I offer a perspective and my experience. You're welcome to disagree, or argue with me. That's encouraged.

You have a 100% right to say stop

At any moment you can turn the temperature down or close a topic entirely. Therapy doesn't have to mean forcing depth or chasing insights. Presence and being witnessed turn out to move people remarkably far.

I keep my own lens clean

I want to see you through a perception that's been wiped of my own smudges. That's what my therapy and my individual and group supervision are for.

The frame outside is firm

Maximum freedom inside the session, a very clear structure around it. A regular setting, weekly or fortnightly, at a fixed time. Payment before the session. Cancellations and reschedules up to 48 hours ahead.

And it's put in writing

If you decide to continue after the first session, I'll send you an informed-consent agreement to sign.

Honest answers

The questions people actually ask

Why is this safe?

I build a sealed, confidential space with no judgement and no scoring. I work strictly on the principle that the client is okay. I don't give advice and I don't decide for you what you should do — I ask questions, get to know you, and reflect back the things that are hard to see from the inside.

Will you definitely help me?

No, not definitely — no psychologist can guarantee that. We work on your wellbeing together, which means change needs you to want it and to do something new in that direction.

I won't be able to help if:

  • You don't take responsibility for your own life
  • Someone made you come to me
  • You want to change another person
What I don't work with

There are things outside my competence, and I'd rather say so before you book than halfway in.

  • Psychiatric diagnoses
  • Depression as the presenting request
  • Suicidal themes and self-harm as the presenting request

If any of that is what you're facing right now, please reach a psychiatrist or a crisis service — that's the right kind of help, and it exists.

The setting

How we work, practically

  • You pick a time that suits you, sign the agreement, and pay for the sessions you've chosen.
  • You join from a private place where nobody can interrupt, see or hear you.
  • Payments aren't refunded. Resistance and the urge to cancel are part of the process.
  • If you're late, the session still ends at the agreed time.
  • Everything is fully confidential.
  • I don't publish client cases and never use counselling clients for promotion.
  • Ending long-term work is discussed in session, with one closing session.

The full terms live in the contract.
